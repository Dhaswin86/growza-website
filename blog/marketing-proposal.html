What should a marketing proposal include?

A strong proposal links deliverables to the goal, defines assumptions, names dependencies and makes measurement explicit.

Pricing · 5 min

The nine things to look for

  1. A diagnosis of the problem in their own words.
  2. Proposed scope and what is excluded.
  3. Concrete deliverables.
  4. Timeline with milestones.
  5. Responsibilities on both sides.
  6. Assumptions and dependencies.
  7. Price and payment schedule.
  8. Success measures.
  9. A clear next step.
Warning sign: a proposal built mostly from generic service descriptions. If your company name could be swapped for another and nothing else would change, it is a brochure.

Exclusions are a feature

A proposal that names what it does not cover is protecting both sides from the argument that scope creep always produces. Vagueness is not generosity—it is deferred conflict.

Measurement before the work starts

Agree what will be measured, from what baseline, and when you will look. Retrofitting success criteria after delivery is how good work gets judged badly—and bad work gets defended.
