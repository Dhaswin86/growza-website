How to write a marketing brief that gets better proposals

Define the outcome, audience, baseline, scope, budget and decision process before asking anyone for a plan.

Projects · 6 min

The six-part brief

  1. State the business outcome.
  2. Describe the target customer.
  3. Share the current baseline and existing work.
  4. Define the required deliverables.
  5. Set the budget and timeline.
  6. Explain how the decision will be made.

A clear brief does not tell the specialist exactly how to solve the problem. It gives them enough context to recommend the right approach.

The baseline matters most. Current traffic, conversion rate or retention is what turns a generic pitch into a specific plan. Withholding it produces vaguer proposals, not better prices.

Why budget belongs in the brief

Hiding the budget does not get you a cheaper quote—it gets you proposals scoped for the wrong company. A range is enough. It tells specialists whether to propose a focused project or a full programme, and it filters out the ones who were never a fit.

Name the decision process

Say who decides, what they care about and when. Specialists prioritise briefs where the path to a yes is visible, and the ones who do not respond were unlikely to stay engaged through delivery.
